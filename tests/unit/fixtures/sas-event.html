The collection of defixiones from Roman Carthage, currently the largest known corpus from the Roman west, has become a focal point of scholarly interest: not only due to the sheer number of tablets, but also because of its rich and varied content, which provides us a wonderful glimpse of daily life in this cosmopolitan, vibrant city. The aim of this talk is two-folded: after dusting off the history of this corpus and examining the biography of several of the scholars who led its early excavation and study, I turn to a series of Greek curse tablets written against charioteers and chariot-horses. Specifically, I will focus on the layout of these texts, which were written in a square spiral layout, proposing a new interpretation for why professional practitioners opted for such a mysterious format.
